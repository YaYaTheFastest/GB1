GRACIE BARRA PRINCIPLES

Founded by Master Carlos Gracie Jr., Gracie Barra is built on the belief that Jiu-Jitsu is more than a martial art — it is a way of life. These principles form the philosophical foundation of every GB school worldwide.

🔴 "JIU-JITSU FOR EVERYONE"
◈ CORE PRINCIPLES
01
JIU-JITSU FOR EVERYONE
Gracie Barra's founding mission: Jiu-Jitsu is accessible to all, regardless of age, gender, or athletic background. GB schools worldwide are welcoming environments where anyone can begin their journey.
"The mat does not care who you were before you stepped on it."
02
RESPECT
Respect for your instructor, your training partners, the academy, and yourself. On the mat, ego is left at the door. The bow before and after each roll is a commitment to mutual safety and dignity.
"Respect your partner. They are your greatest teacher."
03
DISCIPLINE
Consistent training over time is the only path to growth. Discipline means showing up when motivation is low, drilling a technique for the hundredth time, and trusting the process.
"Discipline is doing what needs to be done even when you don't want to."
04
TEAMWORK
You cannot reach your potential alone. GB trains as a team. Your training partners' growth is your growth. Helping a teammate improve makes the entire academy stronger.
"Iron sharpens iron. We grow together."
05
CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT
Jiu-Jitsu cultivates humility, patience, perseverance, and resilience. A belt promotion at GB reflects not just technical skill but the growth of character.
"A black belt is a white belt who never quit."
06
POSITIVITY
The GB environment is one of encouragement, not intimidation. Senior students lead by example. GB rejects the "shark tank" mentality and builds a community where everyone thrives.
"Create the environment you wish you had when you started."
07
BALANCE
Jiu-Jitsu should enhance your life, not consume it. GB promotes balance between training, family, work, and health. A balanced practitioner is a sustainable practitioner.
"The goal is lifelong Jiu-Jitsu."
08
SELF-DEFENSE AS FOUNDATION
Every GB program begins with self-defense. Practical self-defense capability is the original purpose of Jiu-Jitsu and remains the bedrock of the GB curriculum.
"Know how to defend yourself first. Everything else follows."

◈ THE BELT SYSTEM
⬜ WHITE BELT
Focus on survival, basic positions, and building the habit of training. The goal is staying calm under pressure and absorbing fundamentals.
🔵 BLUE BELT
Technical competency with foundational techniques. Can defend, escape, and attack with basic proficiency. Personal game begins to emerge.
🟣 PURPLE BELT
Beginning of mastery. Developed personal game, can teach fundamentals, understands deeper connections between techniques.
🟤 BROWN BELT
Refinement. Fine-tune the game, develop teaching ability. Efficiency replaces athleticism.
⬛ BLACK BELT
A commitment, not a destination. Represents a lifetime of practice, teaching, and contribution to the art.

◎ TECHNICAL CONCEPTS
POSTURE
Alignment of spine and hips. Good posture in guard (top) means straight back, head up, hips forward — resisting being broken down.
BASE
Your stability and balance. A wide, low base resists sweeps and takedowns.
PRESSURE
Using body weight intentionally to control and exhaust the opponent. Good pressure feels heavy and suffocating.
HIP ESCAPE (SHRIMPING)
The most fundamental BJJ movement. Lateral hip motion to create space, recover guard, and escape bad positions.
BRIDGE (UPA)
Explosive hip elevation to escape mount. Combined with trapping an arm and leg, it rolls the opponent.
TECHNICAL LIFT
Safe method of standing from the ground using a posted hand and knee to protect against leg attacks.
FRAMES
Using forearms and hands as structural barriers against opponent's weight. Essential for creating space.
GRIPS
How and where you hold the opponent's gi. Grip control is fundamental — controlling grips controls the entire game.
UNDERHOOK
Inserting your arm underneath the opponent's arm. One of the most powerful control tools in BJJ.
GUARD RETENTION
Preventing the opponent from passing your guard. Requires hip movement, framing, and regripping.
DISTANCE MANAGEMENT
Controlling the space between you and opponent. In self-defense, prevents strikes. In sport, determines available attacks.
TAPPING
Three taps to signal submission and end the roll. Tapping is not failure — it is learning.

POSITIONS & HIERARCHY

BJJ has a positional hierarchy. Understanding where you are on it, and how to improve your position, is the strategic foundation of GB1.

Control → Improve → Submit
⊕ POSITIONAL HIERARCHY
👑REAR MOUNTDominant
Most dominant position. Behind opponent with hooks in — they cannot see your attacks and have limited defense.
AttacksRNC, Bow & Arrow, Arm Lock
ControlSeatbelt, hooks, body triangle
GB1 Weeks9–14
⬛MOUNTTop
Sitting on opponent's torso. Full control of upper body. Opponent bears your full weight.
AttacksArmbar, Triangle, Key Lock, Ezequiel
ControlLow/high/s-mount
GB1 Weeks4, 9, 10, 14
🔲KNEE ON BELLYTop
Knee on opponent's belly creates intense pressure. High mobility for transitions.
AttacksArmbar, transition to mount/back
ControlCollar grip, knee pressure
GB1 Weeks4, 9, 11, 14
🔷SIDE CONTROLTop
Perpendicular control of upper body. Heavy chest pressure with hip blocking prevents escape.
AttacksAmericana, Kimura, transitions
ControlNear arm under, far arm over
GB1 Weeks3, 6, 7, 11
🔵CLOSED GUARD BOTTOMBottom
Legs locked around opponent. Fundamental attacking position — sweep, choke, or attack arms.
AttacksTriangle, Armbar, Omoplata, Sweeps
PrincipleBreak posture first, then attack
GB1 Weeks1–5, 8, 13, 16
🕸️OPEN GUARD FAMILYBottom
Spider, De La Riva, Lasso, Butterfly, Half Guard, X-Guard — each uses different grips/hooks.
GB1Spider (7,13), DLR (8), Lasso (13), Butterfly (15), X-Guard (15)
PrincipleMaintain grips, manage distance
🔶HALF GUARDBottom
Control one of top player's legs. Offensive with underhooks or defensive to prevent full pass.
Key toolsUnderhook, knee shield, deep half
AttacksUnderhook sweep, back take
GB1 Weeks7, 12
